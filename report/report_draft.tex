% ------------------------------------------------------------------ title
\title{FILA-Net: Topology-Aware Instance Reconstruction of Solar Filaments}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ abstract (after the black sentence)
We predict, for every pixel of a $2048\times2048$ H-$\alpha$ image, dense
fields---filament region, centerline, boundary, normalized distance and axial
orientation---with an ensemble of ConvNeXt-T and ConvNeXt-S feature-pyramid
networks, and reconstruct each filament as a separate instance with
centerline seeds, an orientation-consistent repair of broken filaments and a
marker-controlled watershed. A gradient-boosted filter then removes the
instances whose expected contribution to the official Panoptic Quality (PQ)
is negative. Every choice is made on out-of-fold (OOF) predictions with folds
that keep each observing day together, and every calibrated step is
evaluated with nested cross-validation. The nested OOF PQ of the final
pipeline is 0.447, against 0.340 between human annotators on the same
images; the public leaderboard score is 0.39.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ introduction (after the black text)
The official score is the micro-averaged PQ over all annotator label sets,
with a match at IoU $>0.5$: a predicted filament is a false positive for every
annotator who did not draw it, and a filament split into two pieces with
IoU $\le0.5$ each costs two false positives and one false negative. Our
design follows from this: (i) predict complete, unfragmented instances;
(ii) report only instances whose expected contribution is positive, which
we show can be decided instance by instance. Section~\ref{sec:methodology}
describes the pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}), Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}
its out-of-fold evaluation. Code, notebook, configuration and weights:
\url{https://github.com/BFtechlab-flow/sole}.

\paragraph{Data and splits.}
The training set has 707 observations, 1{,}154 annotator label sets and
8{,}199 filaments (411 observations labeled once, 145 twice, 151 three
times). Every label set is a training sample. Observations of the same UT
day show the same Sun from different GONG sites minutes apart (101 training
observations share their day), so we use 5 folds grouped by day: all
observations of a day and all their label sets belong to one fold, and the
OOF score is not inflated by near-duplicates. The assignment is frozen in
the repository (\texttt{configs/folds\_day5.csv}). Only the annotations
provided by the competition are used.

\paragraph{Inputs.}
Each grayscale image becomes four channels: intensity normalized between
its 1st and 99th percentiles, CLAHE~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe}, a ridge
response (difference of Gaussians, $\sigma=1.2$ and $5$) that highlights
thin dark threads, and the normalized radial position, which lets the
network account for limb darkening (Fig.~\ref{fig:inputs}).

\paragraph{Network.}
An ImageNet-initialized ConvNeXt encoder~\cite{liu2022convnext} (via
timm~\cite{rw2019timm}) feeds a feature pyramid~\cite{lin2017fpn} fused at
stride~4 with 128 channels, refined by a multi-dilation context block, a
high-frequency branch and a limb-aware gate, and upsampled to full
resolution. $1\times1$ heads predict the region, the centerline (dilated
skeleton), the boundary, the per-filament normalized distance, the axial
orientation $(\cos2\theta,\sin2\theta)$ of the skeleton, and auxiliary
width, curvature, endpoint, junction and aleatoric-uncertainty
maps~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties}. All targets are derived from the
segmentation polygons only. We train two encoders, ConvNeXt-T
(\texttt{fb\_in1k}) and ConvNeXt-S (\texttt{fb\_in22k\_ft\_in1k}), on the
same folds.

\paragraph{Training.}
The loss combines BCE and Dice on region (0.25, 0.30) and centerline (0.10,
0.15), BCE on the boundary (0.08), a smooth-$L_1$ distance term (0.12),
clDice~\cite{shit2021cldice} for topological continuity (0.05), a cosine
loss on the orientation (0.08) and small weights ($\le0.05$) on the
auxiliary heads. We train on random $1024\times1024$ crops (70\% centered on
a filament) with flips, AdamW (lr $2\cdot10^{-4}$, one warm-up epoch, cosine
decay to $10^{-6}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch 2, mixed precision and
gradient clipping, for 30 epochs. Every two epochs the held-out fold is
predicted in full and scored with the official PQ, and the best epoch is
kept (epochs 22--30; Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}).

\paragraph{Inference.}
Images are processed in $1024$-pixel tiles with $256$-pixel overlap blended
by a Hann window, with test-time augmentation by horizontal and vertical
flips (the orientation field is transformed consistently), and the maps of
the ten fold models (2 encoders $\times$ 5 folds) are averaged.

\paragraph{Instance reconstruction.}
The region probability is thresholded and small components are removed; the
thresholded centerline is skeletonized to obtain seeds. Skeleton endpoints of
different fragments are joined when the gap is short, aligned with the
predicted orientation on both sides, supported by the region probability,
free of boundary evidence and compatible in width (greedy acceptance in order
of cost, without cycles). A marker-controlled
watershed~\cite{beucher1993watershed} on the distance map, penalized at
predicted boundaries, then assigns every foreground pixel to one filament,
giving disjoint instances. Its 14 parameters (thresholds, minimum areas,
repair distances and angles, a minimum mean region probability per instance)
are calibrated on OOF predictions (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}). Masks are
written as COCO RLE~\cite{lin2014coco}.

\paragraph{Learned filament filter.}
Predictions are disjoint, so a ground-truth filament is hit by at most one
of them and removing one prediction changes nothing else. Let $a_j$ be the
sum of the IoUs of the matches of prediction $j$ over the label sets of its
image and $d_j$ the sum, over these label sets, of $1/2$ if $j$ matches
nothing and $h/2$ if it matches $h$ filaments. For a kept set $K$ and $G$
ground-truth filaments,
\[
  \mathrm{PQ}(K)=\frac{\sum_{j\in K}a_j}{G/2+\sum_{j\in K}d_j},
\]
exactly (checked against the official counts on all OOF images), so $j$
should be kept iff $a_j/d_j>\mathrm{PQ}$. We regress
$y_j=a_j/(2d_j)$ with a histogram gradient-boosted regressor
\cite{ke2017lightgbm,pedregosa2011sklearn} (300 trees, 15 leaves) on 32
features of each instance: 25 from the predicted maps (area, elongation,
skeleton length, mean/max probabilities, contrast with a 3-pixel ring,
radial position, rank and number of instances in the image) and 7 from the
H-$\alpha$ image itself (intensity, contrast with its surroundings, ridge
response). Instances with predicted $y$ below a threshold are dropped. Two
candidate sets are compared: the calibrated instances, and a permissive set
(no minimum probability, area $\ge100$ pixels) that the filter thins out.
The filter is cross-fitted on OOF predictions and uses only the image being
segmented.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ evaluation
\paragraph{Strategy.}
Our scorer reproduces the competition's micro PQ: it gives identical
scores to the organizers' evaluation functions and to an independent dense
re-implementation on random multi-annotator scenes with missed, split,
merged and spurious filaments. We report OOF scores: every
training image is predicted by the models of the fold that did not see its
day. Uncertainty comes from 2{,}000 bootstrap resamples of whole
observations. Calibration is nested: for each outer fold, the 14
reconstruction parameters are chosen by coordinate ascent on the other four
folds (300 OOF images, 332 distinct settings evaluated) and scored on it;
the filter's candidate set, model and threshold are likewise chosen by an
inner cross-validation that never sees the outer fold. The leaderboard was
used only as a sanity check, never for a choice.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the OOF scores and Table~\ref{tab:steps} the
gain of each step. The ensemble improves on ConvNeXt-T alone in all five
folds (default post-processing: 0.422, 0.411, 0.437, 0.421, 0.433 against
0.408, 0.410, 0.432, 0.409, 0.422); we chose it on fold~0 before training
the other folds, where a paired bootstrap over its 139 held-out observations
gave $+0.015$ PQ (95\% CI $+0.007$ to $+0.024$) with calibrated
post-processing, against $+0.010$ ($+0.002$ to $+0.019$) for a second
ConvNeXt-T seed. The calibrated reconstruction mainly removes false
positives (FP $3827\to2948$, RQ $0.627\to0.648$) at constant SQ. The filter
adds $+0.009$ nested PQ and improves every outer fold (0.434$\to$0.441,
0.429$\to$0.439, 0.447$\to$0.460, 0.436$\to$0.443, 0.446$\to$0.452); an
oracle filter on the same candidates would reach 0.511, so ranking
candidates is still the largest source of loss. Fig.~\ref{fig:rubric}
shows the rubric distributions: with the tuned reconstruction, 3.7\% of
ground-truth filaments are fragmented, 3.0\% of predictions merge several
filaments, 16.4\% of ground-truth filaments are missed and 16.2\% of
predictions are spurious; matched pairs have a median IoU of 0.68 (human
pairs: 0.63). Fig.~\ref{fig:test} shows predictions on test images and
Fig.~\ref{fig:curves} the training curves. Predicting the
maps of one test image with 10 models and 4 flips takes 49.5\,s on an
NVIDIA T4; reconstruction and filter take 1.3\,s on 4 CPU cores. The
submission has 1{,}043 filaments on the 180 test images.
Component ablations (clDice, orientation head, endpoint repair, flip TTA)
were planned but not run within the GPU budget; their individual
contribution is therefore not measured.

\paragraph{Strengths and weaknesses.}
The dense centerline and orientation fields keep long, curved filaments in
one piece (fragmentation 3.7\%), and the filter turns the PQ trade-off
between missed and spurious filaments into an explicit, per-instance
decision; both are validated without leakage between days. The main
weakness is the choice of which faint features count as filaments: misses
and spurious predictions are equally frequent (16\%), as between human
annotators (Fig.~\ref{fig:inputs}), and the gap to the oracle filter
(0.447 vs.\ 0.511) shows that instance ranking can still improve. The
public leaderboard (0.39) is below the OOF estimate (0.447), which suggests
that the test images differ from the training days; we did not adapt to it.
Possible improvements: global instead of greedy endpoint matching, more
ensemble members, and self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled GONG frames.
